# Another Angle server (hushmallow Beyond)

This small server is the only part of hushmallow that talks to an outside model. It sits between the app and Claude, so the API key never reaches a phone or the Framer site, and it checks every answer before the app sees it.

```
hushmallow app (Pages / Framer / iPhone)
   │  POST /v1/angle  (session token, the person's words, chosen feeling)
   ▼
Cloudflare Worker  ── safety check → Claude (structured JSON) → schema check → quality gate
   │                     (one retry with the reasons; otherwise "use your own engine")
   ▼
scene JSON → the app's cinematic renderer (Zuzu, Thought Mallows, two subtitle lines)
```

It is **not** part of the GitHub Pages site: `tools/build-site.sh` only copies `src/` and the art, and this folder deploys separately through `.github/workflows/another-angle.yml`.

## What happens on each request

1. **Safety first — before any limit.** HTTPS only, and the request must come from an allowed website (`ALLOWED_ORIGINS`). As soon as the body can be read, the words (at most their first 2,000 characters at this point, before any token is checked) are checked against the app's own safety lexicon (a byte-for-byte copy of `hushmallow/src/data/safety-lexicon.js`) plus the server-only additions in `src/safety/extra-lexicon.js`. Anything about self-harm, suicide, someone in danger, abuse, violence, threats, harassment, discrimination or feeling unsafe gets the authored support message straight away — even with a missing or expired token, during a burst, or without `consent` — and the model is never called and no quota is used.
2. **Then the other checks.** Invisible characters are taken out of the words and the earlier angles before any check and before anything is sent (`src/validate.js` `cleanText`: control characters, zero-width and bidi characters, variation selectors, Hangul fillers, Unicode tag characters and the rest of the default-ignorable and format characters), so nothing hidden can split a risk word or carry words past the checks to the model. Each network gets a short burst limit (a network is one IPv4 address, or one IPv6 `/64` block — a single phone or home usually has a whole `/64`, so counting single IPv6 addresses would let one device dodge the limit); the session token must be valid; the body must be well-formed (text 1–600 characters, a feeling and intention from the app's lists, at most four earlier angles, `"consent": true`). Text with no words at all (only emoji or punctuation) gets the "tell me a little more" answer, without the model.
3. **Limits.** Each device that sends a `device_id` (or, later, each subscriber) has a daily allowance (`DEVICE_DAILY_LIMIT`, 20 by default); see `POST /v1/session` for what that does and does not guarantee. The whole service has a daily spending cap (`DAILY_SPEND_CAP_USD`, $10 by default). If the cap is reached the app is told to use its own authored engine instead.
4. **The model.** One request to `claude-opus-5-5` at low effort, with the versioned instructions in `prompts/angle-v5.md` (see "Prompt versions" below) and a strict JSON schema (structured outputs). The person's words are sent inside a marked data block that cannot be closed or used to give instructions. If the model's safety classifier declines, Anthropic's recommended fallback model answers in the same call (`fallbacks: "default"`).
5. **Checking the answer.** The JSON must match the schema exactly (fixed lists of Mallows, reasoning patterns and mechanics). The model's own safety judgement is honoured. Every visible field is then read for harm (`src/safety/index.js` `outputIsSafe`): the lexicon on the words as written, the lexicon again on them turned into the first person ("you should hang yourself" reads as "I should hang myself", "nobody would miss you" as "nobody would miss me"), and second-person rules for means, worthlessness and violence advice the lexicon has no words for ("take all of your pills", "stop eating for a few days", "hit your boss hard"). Any hit: the app is told to use its authored engine, with support kept close, and the model is not asked again (a second draft could word the same harm). Every line the model has written in `eval/runs` passes (`test/output-harm.test.js`). Then a deterministic quality gate checks the two subtitle lines: one short sentence each, 15–35 words together, no line over 22 words, no generic affirmations, empathy clichés, therapy jargon, false reassurance, blame, links or code, nothing stated as fact about someone else's mind, no invented names, days or numbers presented as facts, and no number that changes one of theirs ("three weeks" after their "two weeks" fails wherever it appears; their own numbers, the word count of a phrase they quoted or reported such as "I love you", everyday objects such as a CV, and inside a suggested step a time box, a small count of what to do or a clock time are fine), British spelling, about the person's actual situation, and genuinely different from angles already shown (a follow-up angle must use a reasoning pattern no earlier angle used, because the scene follows the pattern, and must not repeat the meaning-bearing words an earlier angle added — the person's own words and numbers, in any form ("three cancellations" for their "third time"), and framing words such as "fact" or "question" don't count). Restating what the person said happened is never treated as mind-reading — "Your girlfriend forgot your anniversary" passes as it stands, so the person's account is never turned into "may have forgotten" — while a new guess about someone's motives or feelings must be offered as a possibility.
6. **One second chance.** If the gate fails, the model gets exactly one retry with the specific reasons. If that fails too, the app is told to use its authored engine (`src/ultra/reframe-engine.js`) — it never shows a weak answer.

## The API (for the app)

All responses are JSON with `Cache-Control: no-store`. Errors look like `{ "status": "error" | "limit", "error": { "code", "message" } }`; messages are for developers, never for people.

### `GET /v1/health`

`{ ok, service, prompt_version, schema_version, provider, entitlement_mode, invite_required, configured: { signing_key, provider_key }, billing: { configured, webhook, stripe_mode, key_refused?, live_approved, api_version, automatic_tax } }`

`billing` says only whether web subscriptions are set up (all their values, and the `BILLING` Durable Object) and whether the webhook has its signing secret; never a key or a price. The rest are enums and booleans: `stripe_mode` is the accepted key's mode (`"test"`, `"live"`, or `null` when unset or refused); `key_refused` appears only when a key was refused for this stage (`live_key_on_staging`, `test_key_on_production`, `live_not_approved`: the production Worker takes a live key only once `BILLING_LIVE = "approved"` is in its `[vars]`); `live_approved` is that approval; `api_version` is the pinned `STRIPE_API_VERSION` (or `null`); `automatic_tax` is the Stripe Tax switch.

`invite_required` is `true` when a testing session needs one of `TESTER_INVITE_CODES` (only whether, never a code), so a deploy can be checked for invite mode without trying a session.

### `POST /v1/session`

Testing phase body: `{ "device_id": "<random id>" }` (plus `"invite": "CODE"` if `TESTER_INVITE_CODES` is set). Production body: `{ "signed_transaction": "<StoreKit 2 Transaction.jwsRepresentation>" }`. Optional: `platform` (`web`/`ios`), `app_version`.

* `device_id` (testing phase): 16–128 letters, digits, `-` or `_`. The app makes it once — for example 16 bytes from `crypto.getRandomValues`, base64url-encoded — keeps it in local storage, and sends the same value every time. The server never stores or logs it: it is hashed with `IP_HASH_KEY` into the token's subject, so a new session for the same device keeps the same daily allowance. Without a `device_id`, every new session starts a fresh allowance.

Web subscriptions (`ENTITLEMENT_MODE = "billing"` or `"billing+testing"`, see below): the same body, plus the signed-in account's Supabase access token as `Authorization: Bearer <access token>`. The server checks it with the sign-in project and needs an active hushmallow Beyond subscription for that account. In `"testing"`, `"storekit"` and `"both"` the header is never read.

Response: `{ status: "ok", token, token_type: "Bearer", expires_at, source: "testing" | "storekit" | "billing", quota: { daily, remaining, resets_at } }`. `quota` is the allowance of this token's subject: the device (with `device_id`), the subscription (StoreKit), or just this session (neither). Keep the token (it is not secret from the person, but treat it like a password); send it as `Authorization: Bearer <token>`. On a `401` from `/v1/angle`, get a new session. That includes `401 session_superseded` (also on `/v1/feedback`): in `"billing"` only a billing session is accepted, and in `"billing+testing"` only a testing one issued there with a tester code (its token says so, `inv: 1`; none without `TESTER_INVITE_CODES`), so a session issued before the switch is replaced by one that follows the current rules. Limits: 3 new sessions a minute and `SESSIONS_PER_IP_DAILY` (10) a day per network, `SESSION_DAILY_CAP` (500) a day in all; a refusal is `429 session_limit`.

**What the allowance does and does not stop.** In the testing phase the `device_id` comes from the app, so an honest app gets exactly `DEVICE_DAILY_LIMIT` angles a day per device however often it renews its session. Someone who edits the app could send a new id with each session; that is bounded by the per-network session limit, so one network can get at most `SESSIONS_PER_IP_DAILY × DEVICE_DAILY_LIMIT` (10 × 20 = 200) angles a day, and the whole service by `DAILY_SPEND_CAP_USD`. Invite codes (below) close that gap; StoreKit subjects in production are tied to the purchase.

### `POST /v1/angle`

```json
{
  "consent": true,
  "text": "My colleague got promoted instead of me.",
  "emotion": "disappointed",
  "intention": "help-me-feel-steady",
  "previous_angles": [
    { "subtitle_line_1": "…", "subtitle_line_2": "…", "reasoning_pattern": "practical_problem" }
  ],
  "locale": "en-GB",
  "request_id": "optional-8-to-64-chars"
}
```

* `emotion`: any id from `src/data/emotions.js` or a chip word (`angry`, `anxious`, `sad`, `overwhelmed`, `frustrated`, `lonely`, `restless`, `something-else`).
* `intention`: `calm` / `let_go` / `distract` / `steady` / `play` (the chip labels, e.g. `calm-me-down`, are accepted too).
* `text`: exactly what the person typed. Text that is only emoji or punctuation ("😭😭😭") is accepted and answered with the `unclear` fallback (no model call, no quota); only blank text is a `400 empty_text`.
* `previous_angles`: what the person already saw, for "Show me another angle" (up to four). They are checked for instruction-like wording too (and treated as data either way). Each needs both lines; `reasoning_pattern` and `interaction_type` are optional, must come from the lists below and must match each other if both are sent. Send at least one of them: the follow-up must use a different pattern from the last one shown.
* Never send photos: the server rejects unknown fields.

Response: HTTP 200 whenever the server could look at the request — including support and fallback answers. Other codes mean the request did not get that far: `400`/`413`/`415` the body is wrong, `401` the token is missing or expired (get a new session), `403` origin or HTTPS, `429` a limit (daily quota or burst; see below), `500`/`503` the server itself.

```json
{
  "status": "ok",
  "prompt_version": "angle-v5", "schema_version": "scene-v1", "request_id": "…",
  "scene": {
    "emotion": "disappointment", "situation_type": "work", "reasoning_pattern": "practical_problem",
    "known_fact": "…", "possible_assumption": "", "alternative_angle": "…", "practical_next_step": "…",
    "interaction_type": "assemble_step", "mallow_character": "boppo",
    "subtitle_line_1": "That disappointment makes sense.",
    "subtitle_line_2": "Could you ask what specific experience or results would strengthen your next promotion opportunity?",
    "safety_status": "ok"
  },
  "support": null, "fallback": null, "keep_support_close": false,
  "quota": { "daily": 20, "remaining": 19, "resets_at": "…" }
}
```

| `status` | Meaning | What the app does |
|---|---|---|
| `ok` | `scene` is checked and ready | Play the scene for `interaction_type`, reveal the two lines |
| `crisis` / `care` | `support` holds the authored title, message and helplines | Show support (`openSupport(app, {urgent: status === 'crisis'})`); no reframe, no Thought Mallow transformation |
| `fallback` | `fallback.reason` is `quality`, `unclear`, `refusal`, `timeout`, `provider` or `capacity` | Run the app's own safety check first (below), then use the authored engine. `unclear` means "ask for a little more". `fallback.hint` may suggest `{ reasoning_pattern, interaction_type, mallow_character, authored_pattern }` |

`keep_support_close: true` means the words sounded heavy (for example "can't cope"): keep the Support chip visible.

**For the Beyond client (W8): the app runs its own safety check before any authored answer.** A `fallback` only means the model gave no usable answer — it does **not** mean the words were checked and found safe. The server's word list catches a lot, but when the model call fails (timeout, refusal, provider error, spending cap) the model's own safety judgement is missing too. So, before showing anything from the authored engine:

1. Run `checkText(text)` from `src/engine/safety.js` on the person's words first (before even calling the server is best), and on `crisis`/`care` open support (`openSupport(app, { urgent: level === 'crisis' })`) instead of any reframe.
2. Never read a non-200 response, a network error or a timeout as "safe": treat it like a `fallback` and run step 1.
3. Only then hand the words to `src/ultra/reframe-engine.js`.

The server answers crisis and care with support even without a valid token, during a burst limit or without `consent`, so the app can always ask it; but the app's own check must not depend on the server being reachable.

HTTP errors: `400` invalid body (`error.code`, plus `field`), `401` no or bad token, `403` origin not allowed / invite needed / HTTPS required, `413` too large, `415` not JSON, `429` daily limit (`quota`, `Retry-After`) or burst limit (6 angles a minute per network, `Retry-After: 60`), `500` unexpected server error (CORS headers included, so the browser can read it), `503` not configured.

`fallback.hint.authored_pattern` is the closest thinking pattern in the app's own engine (`THOUGHT_PATTERNS` in `src/ultra/frameworks.js`), so the offline path can start from the same idea (`PATTERN_TO_AUTHORED` in `src/vocab.js`):

| `reasoning_pattern` | `authored_pattern` |
|---|---|
| `fact_vs_assumption` | `feelings-as-proof` |
| `catastrophising` | `worst-case-jump` |
| `personalisation` | `spotlight-on-me` |
| `all_or_nothing` | `all-or-nothing` |
| `mind_reading` | `mind-reading` |
| `overgeneralisation` | `always-never` |
| `rumination` | `what-if-loop` |
| `self_criticism` | `big-label` |
| `practical_problem`, `valid_anger` | `null` — not thinking traps; the authored engine's next steps cover them |

### `POST /v1/feedback`

`{ "rating": "helped" | "another_angle" | "not_relevant" | "just_squeeze", "reasoning_pattern"?, "interaction_type"?, "mallow_character"?, "prompt_version"? }` with the same bearer token → `202 { "status": "ok" }`. It has its own burst limit (20 a minute per network), so "Show me another angle" (feedback plus a new angle) never eats into the angle limit. No free text is accepted. It is logged as metadata only (which button, which kind of scene), so you can see which patterns land without anyone's words being kept — the privacy-safe way to "use feedback to improve future suggestions". Nothing is used to train any model.

### Web subscriptions: `/v1/billing/*`

hushmallow Flow and hushmallow Beyond on the web, through Stripe (`src/billing/`). Owner setup, the switch and a test-mode rehearsal are in [`docs/PAYMENTS.md`](../../docs/PAYMENTS.md). Until every value is set, each route answers `503 billing_not_configured` and nothing else changes.

* `POST /v1/billing/checkout` `{ "plan": "paid" | "ultra", "period": "monthly" | "yearly" }` with the account's Supabase access token (`Authorization: Bearer`) → `{ url }`, a Stripe Checkout Session in subscription mode for the configured price, for the account's one Stripe customer (made on its first checkout with only `metadata[user_id]`, `Idempotency-Key: customer:<account>`; if Stripe has deleted it since, a new one, `customer:<account>:<deleted id>`, is kept in its place), `client_reference_id` = the account id, back to `APP_ORIGIN` (`/?billing=success&session_id={CHECKOUT_SESSION_ID}` or `/?billing=cancel`). The price is read from Stripe first (kept 10 minutes) and must be the slot's own (`CHECKOUT_PRICE_RULES`: lookup key `hushmallow.<plan>.<period>`, AUD, recurring every 1 month or 1 year, active, tax-inclusive), else `503 price_misconfigured`. `409 already_subscribed` when the account's record, or Stripe's list of its customer's subscriptions, has one that is `active`, `trialing`, `past_due`, `unpaid` or `paused` (`HELD_STATUSES`: paying an unpaid one's open invoice revives it; changes and payments happen in the portal); a record whose period ended more than a day ago is out of date (its last events lost), so only Stripe's list decides then; `409 payment_pending` when Stripe has one `incomplete`, or the account's last session was paid; `409 customer_conflict` when its customer belongs to another account. The account's last session, still open, is handed back as it is for the same plan, period, price and tax, and expired before a new one is made otherwise. With `STRIPE_AUTOMATIC_TAX` on, the session gets `automatic_tax[enabled]`, `customer_update[address]=auto` and `billing_address_collection=required`; on an API version pin of `2026-03-25` or later, `integration_identifier`. Checkouts for the same account, plan and period within two minutes send Stripe the same `Idempotency-Key`, so two tabs get one Checkout Session.
* `GET /v1/billing/status` with the token → `{ tier: "none" | "paid" | "ultra", status, current_period_end, cancel_at_period_end, ends_at, manageable }`. `ends_at` is when the plan ends if it is set to (Stripe's `cancel_at`, else the period end when it cancels then), `null` while it renews; `cancel_at_period_end` is true only for a cancellation at the period end (a `cancel_at` within a day of it), not for one further out.
* `POST /v1/billing/sync` `{ "session_id": "cs_…" }` with the token (back from Checkout) → the status body, once that session (complete, subscription mode, started by this account) has had its subscription read from Stripe and recorded, as the webhook would. `400 invalid_session` (not a Checkout Session id, or one of the other Stripe mode), `403 not_yours`, `404 session_not_found`, `409 checkout_not_complete`, `409 customer_conflict`, `502 billing_unavailable`.
* `POST /v1/billing/portal` `{}` or `{ "intent": "switch" }` with the token → `{ url }`, Stripe's customer portal (cancel, change plan, card, receipts), back to `/?billing=portal`. With `"switch"` it opens the plan change of the account's subscription (`flow_data[type]=subscription_update`) and answers `{ url, flow: "subscription_update" | "portal" }`: `"portal"` when the subscription gives no access, or Stripe refuses the flow (`400`: the portal does not let customers switch plans) and the home opens instead. `404 no_subscription` before the first subscription.
* `POST /v1/billing/webhook`: Stripe's events. No Origin (it is not a browser); the `Stripe-Signature` header must be an HMAC-SHA256 of `t.payload` with `STRIPE_WEBHOOK_SECRET` from the last 5 minutes. Handled (`EVENTS` in `src/billing/routes.js`): `checkout.session.completed` / `async_payment_succeeded` / `async_payment_failed` (subscription mode only), `customer.subscription.created` / `updated` / `deleted` / `paused` / `resumed`, `invoice.paid` / `payment_failed` / `payment_action_required`; anything else is acknowledged and ignored. Each event reads its subscription back from Stripe and records it as of when it asked, so out-of-order deliveries (or out-of-order answers) cannot leave an old state, and each event id is applied once. When the subscription read has ended (`canceled`, `incomplete_expired`, `unpaid`), the customer's subscriptions are listed and the best one (access, then plan, then period end) is recorded, so an account with a second subscription keeps the one still billing. An event whose `livemode` is not the key's mode is acknowledged and gives nothing (`mode_mismatch`), and on a `404` from Stripe the event's own copy may only take access away.

Accounts are checked by calling `<SUPABASE_URL>/auth/v1/user` with the token and the publishable key (only the hushmallow1 project is accepted; an answer is kept in memory for a minute). Subscriptions live in the `Billing` Durable Object (`src/billing/store.js`): one record per account (Stripe customer and subscription ids, Stripe's status, the plan and period, the period end, whether it cancels at the period end) plus customer → account links. Access is Stripe's `active`, `trialing` or `past_due`, until a day after the period ends; `past_due` for at most 14 days from when the subscription first read it (`PAST_DUE_GRACE_MS`, in step with Smart Retries). Checkout, status, sync and portal share a burst limit (`BILLING_IP_LIMITER`, 20 a minute per network); the webhook is checked by its signature instead.

**Modes.** `"billing"`: `/v1/session` needs an active Beyond subscription; no invite code is accepted. The token's subject is a keyed hash of the account id (the daily allowance is per subscriber, on every device), and it expires with the paid period, at most `BILLING_TOKEN_TTL_HOURS` (24). `"billing+testing"`: that, or one of `TESTER_INVITE_CODES` (a testing session with the testing caps); without codes it is `"billing"`. A cancelled or refunded subscription keeps a session it already has until that token expires (at most a day).

### The ten mechanics

| `reasoning_pattern` | `interaction_type` | The scene | Usual Mallow |
|---|---|---|---|
| `fact_vs_assumption` | `pull_apart` | Pull two connected Thought Mallows apart | Zuzu |
| `catastrophising` | `shrink_projection` | Slowly resize an exaggerated future projection | Loomi |
| `personalisation` | `return_responsibility` | Move imagined responsibility back to where it belongs | Boppo |
| `all_or_nothing` | `reveal_spectrum` | Reveal the spectrum between two extremes | Zuzu |
| `mind_reading` | `open_interpretations` | Open several interpretations, none claimed true | Pipzi |
| `overgeneralisation` | `separate_one` | Lift one event out of a larger collection | Zuzu |
| `rumination` | `let_loop_drift` | Let a looping thought keep moving without grabbing it | Noomi |
| `practical_problem` | `assemble_step` | Assemble one small, doable next step | Boppo |
| `self_criticism` | `soften_harshness` | Soften a harsh verdict without erasing accountability | Boppo |
| `valid_anger` | `find_boundary` | Identify the boundary or response that may be needed | Glitzi |

The server always pairs the mechanic with the pattern, and keeps the Mallow in role (Zuzu is always allowed; Blubi for sadness, Glitzi for anger, Loomi for worry and so on). Lists live in `src/vocab.js`.

## Setting it up (once)

You need a Cloudflare account (the free plan is fine for a first look — see "CPU time" under Costs before inviting testers), an Anthropic API key, and access to this repository's settings on GitHub.

1. **Cloudflare account.** Sign up at cloudflare.com. In the dashboard, open **Workers & Pages** once so your `workers.dev` subdomain is created.
2. **Cloudflare API token.** My Profile → API Tokens → Create Token → use the **Edit Cloudflare Workers** template → Continue → Create. Copy the token.
3. **Cloudflare account ID.** It is shown on the right of the Workers & Pages overview page (or in the URL after `dash.cloudflare.com/`).
4. **Anthropic API key.** In the Anthropic Console (console.anthropic.com) → API Keys → Create Key. While you are there, set a monthly spend limit under Billing as a second safety net.
5. **Add three GitHub secrets (and, recommended, a fourth).** In the repository: Settings → Secrets and variables → Actions → New repository secret:
   * `CLOUDFLARE_API_TOKEN` — from step 2
   * `CLOUDFLARE_ACCOUNT_ID` — from step 3
   * `ANTHROPIC_API_KEY` — from step 4
   * `TESTER_INVITE_CODES` — recommended while you are testing: one or more codes, separated by commas (for example `MALLOW-OCT-7Q2`). Only someone who enters a code in the app can start a session. Without it, anyone who finds the app can use the testing allowance. See "Testing-phase access".
6. **Deploy.** Push to `main` (or Actions → "Another Angle server" → Run workflow). The workflow runs the tests, then deploys. On the first deploy it also creates two random keys on the Worker (`TOKEN_SIGNING_KEY`, which signs session tokens, and `IP_HASH_KEY`, which hashes network addresses for the limits); later deploys keep them. The keys are only created when Cloudflare says the Worker does not exist yet; if the workflow cannot read the Worker's secrets for any other reason (a network blip, a token without the right permission), the deploy stops with an error instead of replacing the keys, which would sign every tester out — just re-run it. Until the Cloudflare secrets exist the deploy step is skipped with a notice and nothing else breaks.
7. **Find the address.** The deploy log prints `https://hushmallow-another-angle.<your-subdomain>.workers.dev`. Opening `…/v1/health` in a browser shows a 403 (browsers send no allowed origin); `curl -H 'origin: https://ramkumarjothi1982.github.io' …/v1/health` shows `"ok":true`.
8. **Point the app at it.** The Beyond client reads the endpoint from `src/app/config.js`.
9. **Check the logs once.** In the Cloudflare dashboard, open Workers & Pages → `hushmallow-another-angle` → Logs, make one request from the app, and confirm each log line holds only the fields listed in "What is logged" below — no IP address, location or request headers.
10. **Check the CPU time once.** In the same Logs view (or Workers & Pages → the Worker → Metrics → CPU time), look at `cpu_ms` for the first few `/v1/angle` requests, especially the first one after a deploy. See "CPU time" under Costs for what to do if it is near or over the limit.

To change a setting (limits, cap, allowed websites, model), edit `[vars]` in `wrangler.toml` and push. If the site moves to its own domain or the Framer domain needs direct access, add it to `ALLOWED_ORIGINS`.

### Testing-phase access

**Recommended: use invite codes.** Add the `TESTER_INVITE_CODES` repository secret (step 5) and redeploy, or set it directly with `npx wrangler secret put TESTER_INVITE_CODES`. The app then sends the tester's code as `invite` when it starts a session; a missing or wrong code gets `403`. Every deploy without codes shows a notice in the Actions log.

Without codes (`ENTITLEMENT_MODE = "testing"`), anyone using the app can get a session. Abuse is bounded — 3 new sessions a minute and 10 a day from one network (an IPv4 address or an IPv6 `/64`), 500 new sessions a day overall, 20 angles a day per `device_id` (an edited app can send a new id with each session, so the real ceiling per network is 10 sessions × 20 = 200 angles), and the daily spend cap — but someone determined could use up the day's 500 sessions or the spend cap and lock real testers out until midnight UTC. Many phones on mobile data share one IPv4 address, so a per-network limit alone cannot tell them apart. Invite codes avoid both problems.

### Before you sell Beyond (production entitlement)

On the web: Stripe, `ENTITLEMENT_MODE = "billing"`; see [`docs/PAYMENTS.md`](../../docs/PAYMENTS.md). In the App Store app, StoreKit:

`src/auth/storekit.js` verifies StoreKit 2 signed transactions: Apple's ES256 signature, the certificate chain up to **Apple Root CA – G3** (pinned by its SHA-256 fingerprint), Apple's marker extensions, your bundle id, a Beyond product id (`hushmallow.ultra.monthly` / `.yearly`), environment, expiry and revocation. **It has only been tested against certificate chains generated in the tests to match Apple's structure — never against a real App Store receipt.** Before launch:

1. Set `APPLE_BUNDLE_ID` and `ENTITLEMENT_MODE = "storekit"` (or `"both"` during the switch-over) in `wrangler.toml`; set `APPLE_ENVIRONMENTS = "Sandbox"` for TestFlight testing, then `"Production"`.
2. From a Sandbox build, send `Transaction.jwsRepresentation` for a Beyond purchase to `/v1/session` and confirm you get a token; try an expired and a non-Beyond purchase and confirm they are refused.
3. Remove the testing hosts from the app's `TEST_PURCHASE_HOSTS` (see `src/app/config.js`).
4. Consider App Store Server Notifications for refunds: today a refunded subscription keeps working until its session token expires (at most 24 hours, `STOREKIT_TOKEN_TTL_HOURS`).

## Costs

Prices for `claude-opus-5-5`: $4 per million input tokens, $20 per million output tokens; the cached instructions are read at $0.20 per million.

* **A typical angle costs about 2p ($0.02)**: ~6,200 cached instruction tokens (angle-v5), a few hundred tokens of the person's words, and up to ~1,000 output tokens (short internal reasoning at low effort plus the JSON). A regenerated answer roughly doubles that.
* **Daily cap:** `DAILY_SPEND_CAP_USD = 10` (≈ 400–500 angles a day). Before each call the worst case (~$0.31) is reserved against the cap, then replaced by the real cost. Set it to `0` to pause Another Angle instantly; the app falls back to its authored engine.
* **Per device:** 20 angles a day (`DEVICE_DAILY_LIMIT`) for each `device_id` (see `POST /v1/session`). An angle is given back when nothing came from the model: a timeout, a refusal, a provider error, the spending cap, or no API key. An answer the model did write still counts even if the person does not see it, because it cost a model call: one the quality checks held back (`quality`), or a reading that there was too little to work with (`unclear`). That stops one device spending the model budget for free. Support answers (crisis or care) and emoji-only text never count.
* **Second safety net:** the monthly spend limit in the Anthropic Console.
* Cloudflare's free Workers plan covers 100,000 requests a day; Durable Objects (the counters) are included on the free plan within its limits.
* **CPU time.** The free plan allows 10 ms of CPU per request; waiting for the model does not count, only the server's own work. Measured on a slow shared test machine, a warm request costs about 2–3 ms (5–6 ms with a regeneration), plus about 1–2 ms for the output check's first-person reading of each visible field (15 Oct 2026; p50 2.1 ms, p95 4.2 ms for the whole output check over the 128 scenes in `eval/runs`, against 1.2 / 1.8 ms before). Compiling the few hundred safety and quality-check patterns is the expensive part, so the Worker does it once while it starts up (`warmSafety()` and `warmGate()`), not on the first request: the first request after start-up dropped from about 120–170 ms to about 4 ms in the same measurement. Start-up has its own, larger allowance, and Cloudflare refuses a deploy that exceeds it. Real Cloudflare machines are faster than the test machine, but this has not been measured on a live deploy. After the first deploy (setup step 10), if `cpu_ms` is close to 10 on ordinary requests, or people see error 1102 ("Worker exceeded resource limits"), move to **Workers Paid** ($5 a month, 30 s of CPU per request by default) — nothing else needs to change.

## The quality evaluation

`hushmallow/tools/angle-eval.mjs` runs 49 inputs — 33 realistic situations (relationships, work, anxiety, jealousy, shame, self-criticism, disappointment, rumination, uncertainty), one too-short input, three safety probes, two prompt-injection probes and 10 held-out situations, plus nine "another angle" follow-ups (one case asks for a third angle) — through the same pipeline against the real model, with the Worker's production timeouts and retries (15 s per attempt, 28 s per answer). A second model then grades each answer for relevance, accuracy, concision, actionability, respect, safety and novelty.

The three safety probes skip the word-list check, so the report shows whether the model would have flagged them on its own (the word list's verdict is printed beside it). The report also lists every draft the quality gate rejected, with the lines, the failing field and the reason, so a good answer refused by mistake is as visible as a weak one caught.

The judge is a different model from the one that writes the answers (`claude-sonnet-5-5` grades `claude-opus-5-5`), so the eval is not a model marking its own homework. `--judge-model claude-opus-5-5` is allowed, and the report header then warns that the scores may be generous.

After the first real run, check `work-embarrassed`, `work-pitch` and `shame-nephew`: they must come back as an angle (`ok`), not as support. If the model sends one-off workplace or family friction to `care`, narrow the "care" wording in the prompt further.

```
cd hushmallow/server/another-angle
ANTHROPIC_API_KEY=sk-ant-… node ../../tools/angle-eval.mjs        # prints the cost estimate, sends nothing
ANTHROPIC_API_KEY=sk-ant-… node ../../tools/angle-eval.mjs --yes  # runs it (estimate about $0.85 for 49 cases, worst case about $12; runs 1 and 2, 39 cases, cost $0.59 each)
node ../../tools/angle-eval.mjs --mock                             # free dry run of the harness
```

The report lands in `eval/reports/angle-eval-<date>.md` (and a `.json` beside it, not committed; it contains the model's answers and rejected drafts, so keep it private). Start with "Fix before expanding": the brief asks for weak answers to be fixed before anything grows. Then read "Gate rejections" for false alarms, and the latency line for answers slower than the 15 s / 28 s limits. The summary also shows the subtitle length against the 16–28 aim, how often line 2 proposes a step or asks an answerable question (a rough word-pattern reading, not a grade), and whether each "another angle" used a new pattern.

**Held-out cases.** The last 10 inputs in `eval/cases.js` (`heldOut: true`) were added after run 2. No real answer to them has been seen, so no prompt change and no gate fix was made from the model's answers to them, and none is a prompt example. They are not wholly held out from the gate, though: the gate's third review wrote answers by hand on their wording, and some of its rules were checked against those ("in July" for ho-results, "a five-minute sorry" for ho-shouted, and before that "if she cancels a fourth time" for ho-cancelled, a test that now uses a sentence outside the eval). The gate's fixes were mostly checked against the real answers to the first 39 inputs (runs 1 and 2), so its pass rates on those flatter it. The report prints every measure for the held-out cases and for the 39 earlier ones side by side ("Held-out and earlier cases"): the earlier column has the same inputs as runs 1 and 2, so read it, not the overall summary, against their summaries; the held-out column is the fairer reading of how the gate generalises, but its actionability is not comparable (grief and fresh heartbreak get no task by design). They also measure what angle-v2 and v3 changed but runs 1 and 2 could not: no forced action on grief, fresh heartbreak or a worry out of their hands, fair anger that needs a boundary, a third angle (`followUp: 2`), and an amount and a day the person gave themselves.

`--prompt angle-v2` runs the eval with an older prompt file (the default is the server's `PROMPT_VERSION`), so two prompts can be compared on the same inputs and judge. The request wording follows the version too (angle-v1 never defined the lenses, so a v1 run gets v1's one-line "another angle" request), but the quality gate is always the current one. So `--prompt` compares prompts under today's gate; the measurement of each prompt *with its own gate* is the run that introduced it: run 1 for angle-v1, run 2 for angle-v2 (`eval/runs/`). To try a prompt change, copy the current prompt to the next version (`angle-v6.md`), edit it, point `src/index.js` and `PROMPT_VERSION` at it, run the eval, and compare reports.

**Replaying the gate (free).** `npm run replay:gate` re-runs the current quality gate alone over every answer of a recorded run — the accepted answers and the drafts it rejected — and lists which rejections would no longer happen, which still would (and why), and any accepted answer that would now fail. With no argument it replays the whole replay set, every `eval/runs/run*-answers.json` (runs 1, 2 and 3); `npm run replay:gate -- eval/runs/run2-answers.json` replays one run, and any later report's `.json` works too. When only the Actions log is at hand (the artifact needs a GitHub login to download), `npm run extract:log -- <job-log.txt> <out.json>` turns the report printed in the log back into that JSON; all three fixtures were made that way. From run 4 the report also names each rejected draft's reasoning pattern and, under each failure, what the regeneration was told (`- told: …`), and the extractor keeps both; for older runs a follow-up that ended in a fallback has no recorded pattern, so the replay assumes the first one no earlier angle used. `test/gate-run1.test.js`, `test/gate-run2.test.js` and `test/gate-run3.test.js` turn the three runs into regression tests; `test/gate-review3.test.js` holds the third review's probes and the verb × noun × count and verb × time-span matrices.

## The real-model check

A controlled, capped run of the real model through the server's real HTTP app and the real built site, in GitHub Actions (`.github/workflows/another-angle-real.yml`, with the `ANTHROPIC_API_KEY` repository secret). Nothing is deployed: the harness (`tools/local-harness.mjs`, `PROVIDER=anthropic`) runs on the runner at `127.0.0.1:8790`, and the site, built in CI mode by `tools/build-site.sh`, is served at `127.0.0.1:8000` and pointed at it with `?angleDev=8790`.

* **API level** (`scripts/real-check.mjs api`), straight HTTP, bypassing the app: health and session; the error contract (empty, oversized and malformed bodies, no session, a forged token, a wrong origin, a burst over the rate limit); support that nothing can get round (no token, no consent, oversized, zero-width characters, a tripped limit); three obfuscated risk sentences only the model can read; every case in `eval/real-cases.json` (status, safety source, gate result, the two lines, latency); a second harness on `:8791` with an invalid key (the fallback path, no cost); and no words in the server log.
* **Browser level** (`../../tools/beyond/real-client.cjs`): the consent card first; "Not now" sends nothing; Accept → the question → "Another angle" → a real angle with both of its lines on screen; "Just be with it", a crisis sentence and a care sentence send nothing; the delete-data sheet clears the phone's Another Angle data; 0 console errors; nothing leaves the runner. Screenshots go in the artifact.
* **Report** (`scripts/real-check.mjs report`): `report.md`, with the per-case table and the inputs, the model's lines, totals, cost, latency p50/p95 and every failure verbatim, goes only in the run's artifact (`another-angle-real`, with `api.json`). The repository is public, and so are its Actions logs and step summaries: what the script prints and the step summary (`summary.md`) hold ids, groups, verdicts, counts, costs and latencies only, never an input sentence or a line the model wrote. **Artifacts of a public repository can be downloaded by any signed-in GitHub user** until they expire (14 days here), so the artifact is not private either: delete a run's artifact (Actions → the run → Artifacts) once it has been read. Anything committed here, `eval/real-cases.json` included, is public too, so a case set meant to stay unseen (held out) cannot live in this repository. A miss (risk read as an angle), false support, an error or a failed step fails the run; a fallback is listed, never hidden.

**Cost.** About $0.60–$0.90 a run (about 30 model requests at ~$0.02), plus ~$0.02 for each added case that reaches the model. Hard caps: the script counts model calls and cost from the usage the harness logs (priced by `src/cost.js`) and stops before one more worst-case request (~$0.32) could pass `--max-calls` (80) or `--max-usd` ($4), keeping room for the browser's one real angle; the harness's `DAILY_SPEND_CAP_USD` is set to the same amount. Without `--yes`, a real provider only gets the estimate.

**Run it:** append a line to `eval/REAL` on a `claude/**` branch (a line `--max-usd 2` or `--max-calls 40` there sets the caps), or Actions → "Another Angle real-model check" → Run workflow (once the workflow is on `main`). **Cases:** `eval/real-cases.json`, synthetic sentences only (15 ordinary, 10 safe edge cases; held-out risk cases go in as `"group": "heldout-risk"` with `"expect": ["care", "crisis"]`). **Free dry run** on the mock model, from this folder:

```

node scripts/real-check.mjs api --log /tmp/aa.log --out /tmp/real --expect-provider mock
HM_AUTH_MODE=ci bash ../../tools/build-site.sh /tmp/site dry && (node ../../tools/auth/pages-serve.mjs /tmp/site 8000 &)
node ../../tools/beyond/real-client.cjs --site http://127.0.0.1:8000 --dev 8790 --out /tmp/real
node scripts/real-check.mjs report --out /tmp/real --log /tmp/aa.log
```

The mock's lines are placeholders, so a dry run proves the plumbing, never the quality or the model's own safety judgement. Not covered: the Worker on Cloudflare (its Durable Object ledger, rate-limit binding, production origins and per-device limit), iOS Safari, and real people's words.

Word counts were already right (contractions and hyphenated words count once); numbers such as "1,000" and "3.5" and abbreviations such as "e.g." now count as one word too.

**The gate after review (with angle-v3).** The first round of fixes went too far in two places, and both are back: (1) *Numbers that change the person's own.* Time boxes counted anywhere in line 2, after "after", "for" or "about", so "After three weeks of silence…" passed for someone who had waited two. Now a number counting the same unit as one of theirs (minutes, hours, days, nights, weeks, months, years, times/tests/attempts) must be theirs, wherever it sits; an ordinal may name an earlier one ("your second test" of three), and the next attempt may be looked at ahead ("before booking test number four"). Inside a suggested action only, a time box after a forward-looking verb ("spend twenty minutes", "wait 48 hours", "for the next hour"), a small count of what to do ("three things", "two or three people", "say them aloud three times") and a clock time *with* its marker ("until 9 tonight", "at 7.30"; "at 6" alone is a fact) are left out; a vantage point counts only when it looks ahead ("in five years' time", "six months from now", "in five years, will…"), never "after five years"; and a count followed by a clause about the past ("the four strengths your manager praised") is a fact again. Digits, words and ordinals are one value ("1,000" is "a thousand", "5" is "five" and "fifth"). Days, months and holidays must be theirs unless they say when a suggested step should happen ("by Friday"). (2) *Generic lines that name a pronoun.* A pronoun for the person they named no longer counts as engaging with the situation; contacting that person does ("check with him in person"). "Slot", "chat" and "conversation" left the synonym lists ("Every conversation is a chance…" is not about their talk); "a conversation" or "the chat" counts only when the person reported a talk ("we need to talk", "wants to see me", "wouldn't say why") and the line is not a general remark. Also: line 2 and the step may restate what the person said happened ("Since she forgot the day, you could…"), but a mind word joined to it ("…noticed and thinks you're careless") is still a claim; a harsh label is checked at each use, so a frame covers only the use it ends on; "like proof that you're stupid" is a frame; "only she knows" leaves her reasons open; "as a friend" is a human claim only when it introduces a speaker; plurals of listed words meet them ("courses" meets "university"); a fallback after a repeated angle no longer hints the repeated pattern (the client would skip it), and feedback is counted against the prompt version of the scene being rated.

Replays after these changes: run 1, all 38 accepted answers still pass and the same 11 false rejections still pass (the 3 over 35 words still fail); run 2 (angle-v2, 40 accepted answers, never used for the first fixes), all 40 still pass, and 3 of its 6 rejections were false alarms that now pass ("say them aloud three times" in a step; "only she knows" and "value you as a friend"; "before booking test number four" after a third fail, with "like proof that you're stupid" in the same draft). The 3 drafts over 35 words still fail. The next attempt is allowed only when it is looked at ahead ("before booking test number four", "if she cancels a fourth time"); "you've failed a fourth time" or "after a fourth test" still fail. Every probe from the review is a test (`test/gate-run1.test.js`, `test/gate-run2.test.js`).

**The gate after its third review (with angle-v4).** Two problems pulling in opposite directions, and the paragraph above is superseded where they differ. (1) *Invented counts about other people passed.* Every line ending in a question mark was read as a suggestion, so "Which of the four friends who came would you see again soon?" passed for "Only three people came to my birthday drinks", and "who came", "who did not come" or "in that meeting" never read as the past. Now a sentence is a suggestion only from where it starts proposing something: an imperative clause ("Update your CV…", "…; now knock and offer him…"), a question that asks the person to do or choose ("Could you…?", "Which… would you…?", "Which three things did you love most…?"), or a condition just before one ("If someone mentions it on Monday, could you…?"). A question about the situation ("Did the six people in that meeting…?", "Were the three who laughed…?") is not one, so a count in it must be theirs. Inside a suggestion a count is a fact again when the words around it place it in the past: a relative clause ("the four friends who came", "the ten people who did not come"), a place in the situation ("the six people in that meeting"), someone else's act ("the four strengths your manager praised"), a past verb straight after it ("two friends came"), or "the last", "those" or "of the" before it. Choosing from their own past is still a step ("the three things you loved most", "three things that went well", "the three things that made you love it"). People are a unit now (people, friends, guests, colleagues…): "four friends", or "two friends came", after their "three people" is a changed number, while "invite two friends round" is a step. "Two" is no longer free when it is one of those facts ("the two people who laughed"), a length of time outside a step ("two days of silence") or occasions they never gave ("twice this week"). (2) *Ordinary steps were refused.* The allowance worked only for a few listed verbs and nouns. Now, inside a suggestion, any small count (up to ten) before a plural noun with up to two words between ("three cheap meals", "your next five spends"), any time span ("for fifteen minutes", "over three evenings", "two evenings this week", "a twenty-minute walk", "a three-day buffer") and "twice" are about the future, unless a past or perfect verb leads up to the span ("you've waited for three weeks"). The person's own units are checked first, on the whole sentence, so none of this can hide "for three weeks" after their "two weeks": only a forward-looking time box ("give it another three weeks", "spend twenty minutes"), a vantage point ahead, a count of people to contact and a repeat ("say it three times") are left alone there. The next occasion, imagined, is theirs ("You checked twice; the third check is happening only in your head"), and a month can say when a step happens ("in July"). When a number fails, the regeneration is told why it read as a fact ("\"three colleagues who saw\" presents it as part of what happened…", "it comes before the suggestion…") instead of the old, contradictory "a count inside a step is fine".

Also from the third review: *names* — the first word of a sentence is checked too: a common given name, or a capitalised word followed by what a person does ("Sarah may still be…", "Tom might need a day", "Ella's silence…"), that is not in their text is an invented name; pronouns, roles, days, Mallows and everyday openers ("Thursday needs…", "Silence might mean…") are not. *Other minds* — "loved", "enjoyed" and "needs space / time / you" are mind words; "does not change how much she values you" is a claim (the verb takes the feeling as given), while "does not tell you how much…" still leaves it open; a mind word joined to something they did ("Your sister crossed a line and knew it") is a claim in every field. *Engagement* — the person's own word counts on its own; a word that only shares a group with theirs and fits almost any line ("answer", "phone", "silence", "result", "work", "plans", "call", "class", the speech words), a stop word ("like you", "see them") or an everyday word ("tonight", "choice", "first", "give") needs a second hit beside it; words inside an idiom ("work out", "your call", "the answer you need", "tell yourself", "talk it through") do not count; "head" left the boss group ("your head is doing a lot of guessing"); and their own compound counts ("first choice" for "first-choice"). Of eight generic line pairs, at least one passed for 28 of the 45 non-probe eval inputs; now 3 do, and in each the person's own word sits in the generic line ("imagine" for "imagining", "result" for "results"), which a word check cannot tell apart.

Checked after these changes: run 1 (38 accepted) and run 2 (40 accepted) all still pass, the same rejections clear (11 of 14 and 3 of 6) and the same over-35-word drafts still fail; of 30 good v3-style answers written for the review, 1 is refused (dis-flat: no word from their situation), against 4; time spans in steps, 0 of 12 refused (5 before); counts before everyday nouns, 0 of 80 (71 before), and 0 of 30,540 in a wider verb × noun × count × input matrix; invented counts in questions and relative clauses, all caught but 4 whose number happens to be the person's own (their "three people"; "I love you" is three words). The gate's cost is unchanged within this machine's noise (1.2–1.8 ms per call on run 2's answers, before and after).

**What the novelty check does and does not measure.** The check on meaning-bearing words was set so that none of run 1's real follow-ups would fail (they share at most one such word with the angle before), and the same holds for run 2's. It catches near-verbatim paraphrases only: it would not have rejected any follow-up the judge scored 3/5, nor run 2's 2/5 follow-up (the same point, "new friends sit beside old ones", reached through a friend's eyes), and a paraphrase in synonyms passes ("His quiet so far has not given you his reply" after "Silence after words that big has not told you his answer yet": "answer" is a framing word, and a word list does not know "quiet" is "silence", so they share none). Counting synonym groups as shared meaning was tried and left out: with these thresholds it changes nothing measured, and lowering them would reject genuinely different follow-ups that share an action ("ask… tonight"). So any novelty gain in run 3 will come from the prompt, the rule that every follow-up uses an unused pattern and (from angle-v4) the request naming each earlier angle's lens, not from this check; the judge's novelty score is the measure.

**The gate after eval run 3 (with angle-v5).** Run 3's three fallbacks all began with a false alarm, and each regeneration then failed too; the fixes are general and pinned in `test/gate-run3.test.js` beside the true positives they must keep. An independent review then probed the first fixes with 92 new lines and found 40 that the angle-v4 gate caught and the fixes let through; each rule was tightened at its root and those probes are pinned too ("review:" tests). (1) *A repetition count inside a step* ("Tonight, say your opening two sentences out loud three times"): the words between the verb and the count may be only what the action is done to (one noun phrase: "your opening lines", "it", "that"), particles ("out loud", "aloud", "again", "slowly") and phrases led by a preposition ("with a friend", "past the mirror", "of your notes"). Anything that starts another clause ends it, and the count then goes with that clause's verb, the nearer one: a wh-word, a subject or an auxiliary ("check why it went wrong three times", "the lines you rehearsed three times"), or, right after a noun, a second determiner or possessive ("the comments your boss makes three times a week"), a person ("the plans people cancel") or a verb form ("the trains arriving late", "the report redone", "the letter slid under your door"). The count must not follow a determiner ("the three times you froze" counts occasions) and no clause may follow it. A count after a clause about a noun is a fact, and the message quotes it ("the friend who cancelled four times" describes what happened); with a habitual frequency after it ("the bins overflowing three times this month", "the door your flatmate leaves open twice a week") it is one even for "twice", which is otherwise free in a step ("Ring her twice this week"). "Practise saying it…" and "Say "I can do this"…" now start a step. (2) *"They" that means a thing* ("…until they feel familiar", about the lines): "they" is a thing only when all three hold: what follows is on a short list of what a thing is said to feel or need ("feel familiar", "easier", "natural", "smaller", "lighter", "less like a script", "second nature", "need more practice"), so "they feel relieved", "they feel generous", "they feel confident in you" or "they feel the miss was bad luck" are people's minds whatever came before; no person is named anywhere earlier in the sentence (a person word, a name, "who" or an owner: "Your colleagues have seen the slides, and they…" is people, though "the slides" is nearer); and a plural thing heading a noun phrase comes before it ("your opening lines", "the worries", "Thursday's slides"), in the sentence or, for line 2, in line 1. A "they" with nothing before it is people ("They feel lighter after a loss…"). (3) *"Who cares" in a kind reframe* ("…tells you who could make that evening, not who cares"): "who cares" is a shrug wherever it stands ("In the end who cares how many came?", "Three people came and who cares about the rest?", "Who cares about you crying at work?", "Who on earth cares…?"); only a "who cares" that names the people who care is kind, and only right after "not", "of" or "about", a verb that shows, finds out or asks it ("shows you who cares", "find out who cares", "ask yourself who cares") or the person it describes ("someone who cares"), and "who cares most" in a real either/or question. "You should know that / by now…" joined the rule; "You're overreacting", "It's really not a big deal", "Just get over it", "Obviously…" and "Calm down" still fail. (4) *The third angle of ho-cancelled*: neither draft repeated a point (the log does not record the drafts' patterns; with any pattern no earlier angle used, as the request asked, both fail the old gate and pass the new one). The first failed the whole-line backstop on line 1 against angle 2's line 1 (Jaccard 0.60), but the three shared words were all the person's own (three, cancelled, month) and the line makes a different point ("a lot to carry" against "would hurt almost anyone", with a new question in line 2); the second failed the meaning check on "three", "suggest", "plan", where "three" is their "third time" — without it, two shared words at 0.17, under both thresholds. So the person's own numbers count as theirs in any form, and on the person's own words alone the whole-line checks count only a restatement: four or more of their words shared at the usual threshold ("In front of the team, your boss criticised the report you wrote" after "Your boss criticised your report in front of the whole team": five at 0.71), or near-verbatim (Jaccard 0.8). A line sharing a word beyond theirs is checked as before. None of the 20 follow-ups the gate accepted in runs 1–3 changes (the closest line pair among them is 0.50, sharing four of the person's words); a restatement said twice, a line sharing new words with an earlier one, a paraphrase of the same point and any pattern an earlier angle used all still fail. *Hedges*: "often" and "usually" now hedge a claim after them ("A slow reply often means she is busy"), as angle-v5 asks; after a claim they hedge nothing ("She is upset with you, as she often is" fails). *Regeneration feedback*, which recovered none of the three: every content rule now quotes the words that tripped it (the second dis-birthday draft kept "not who cares" because the message listed other words); `claims_others_minds` quotes the claim and, where "they" has a feeling or need a thing could also have ("they feel familiar"), says to name the thing if a thing is meant (not for "They're probably embarrassed"); a number in or near a step is told that a step may count what to do, tied to the action, but must not restate or add facts, and a count after a clause about a noun is quoted as what happened; a repeat names the line that is close and the earlier angle, lists only the lenses no earlier pattern used, and says the person's own details may stay.

Checked after these changes: replaying runs 1–3, every accepted answer still passes (38, 40 and 50); the rejections that clear are run 1's same 11 of 14 and run 2's same 3 of 6 (the 3 + 3 over 35 words still fail), and all 6 of run 3's; the third review's probes are unchanged (0 of 30,540 matrix steps refused; the same 56 of 60 invented counts caught; 0 of 20 earlier mismatches), and the gate's cost is unchanged within noise (about 1.2 ms per call).

## What is logged, and what is kept

**Never written to logs or storage by this server:** the person's words, the model's lines, session tokens, IP addresses, device ids, and what kind of safety concern was detected.

**What the server logs (Cloudflare Workers Logs, metadata only):** one line per request with: time, route, HTTP status, outcome (`ok`/`care`/`crisis`/`fallback`), fallback reason, latency, attempts, quality-gate result and failure codes, reasoning pattern, mechanic, Mallow, scene emotion and situation type, the chosen feeling and intention ids, how many earlier angles were sent, a length bucket (short/medium/long), the safety level (`none`/`concern`/`care`/`crisis`, never the category), whether the text looked like an injection attempt, model name, token counts, cost, remaining quota; and, for feedback, which of the four buttons was pressed for which pattern, mechanic and Mallow. `src/log.js` drops anything else, and the tests check that no input text reaches the logs.

**What Cloudflare itself records:** Cloudflare's *invocation logs* — which would store each request's headers and Cloudflare's location data (city, postcode, coordinates, network) beside the lines above — are switched off in `wrangler.toml` (`[observability.logs] invocation_logs = false`), and so are traces. What remains is the server's own lines plus the metadata Cloudflare attaches to each line (such as the time, the Worker's name and version and whether the request succeeded). The exact set is Cloudflare's and was not inspected on a live deploy when this was written, so check it once after the first deploy (setup step 9), and again if you change plans or settings. Cloudflare keeps Workers Logs for a few days; its pricing page lists the period for each plan (sources disagreed between 3 and 7 days on the free plan when this was written; 7 on the paid plan), so confirm it before quoting a number on the privacy screen. Separately, like any web host, Cloudflare's network handles the visitor's IP address to deliver the request and protect against attacks; that is covered by Cloudflare's own privacy policy, not by anything this server keeps.

**Stored by the server:** with web subscriptions on, one record per subscribing account in the `Billing` Durable Object (see "Web subscriptions"): Stripe's customer and subscription ids, its status, the plan and period, the period end and whether it cancels then; plus which account each Stripe customer belongs to, and the ids of Stripe events already applied (30 days). Never card details, emails, names, words or feelings: Stripe keeps the payment details. Otherwise daily counters only — angles per session subject (a keyed hash of the device id, the purchase, or a random id; never the raw device id), new sessions per hashed network (an IPv4 address or an IPv6 `/64`, keyed with `IP_HASH_KEY`, never the raw address), total spend — in a Durable Object. A day's counters are deleted two days after the day ends (00:00 UTC on the third day): on the next write to that object, or by the object's alarm if nothing is ever written there again, so an idle device's count does not outlive it (`src/limits/ledger.js`; the in-memory ledger of the tests and the local harness runs the same alarms).

**Sent to Anthropic:** the person's text, the chosen feeling and intention, and the earlier angle lines for "another angle"; plus an opaque hashed tag (`metadata.user_id`) so Anthropic can spot abuse without knowing who anyone is. The tag is a keyed hash of the session subject and the UTC day, so it changes every day: one phone's requests share a tag within a day, never across days. No photos, account details or device ids. Whatever the person types, including any names, is sent as written. Anthropic does not use commercial API inputs and outputs for model training (Commercial Terms, effective 17 June 2025: "Anthropic may not train models on Customer Content from Services"), unless the organisation opts in to its Development Partner Program. **Retention** (checked 2026-10-12 against Anthropic's live API data-retention docs and its privacy centre, the latter only through search excerpts): commercial API inputs and outputs are deleted within 30 days by default; content flagged by Anthropic's automated trust and safety systems can be kept for up to 2 years (classification scores for up to 7), and anything the law requires is kept. There is no primary source for an earlier note here that the default fell to 7 days; 'within 30 days' is the figure the app uses. The model in use (`claude-opus-5-5`) is not one of Anthropic's Covered Models, so their 30-day minimum does not apply to it. The approved consent wording is in `hushmallow/docs/integration/DECISIONS.md`. Zero data retention can be arranged with Anthropic.

**Deletion:** because the server keeps no content, there is nothing per person to delete here. Rotating `TOKEN_SIGNING_KEY` (move the old value to `TOKEN_SIGNING_KEY_PREVIOUS` first if you want existing sessions to keep working) signs everyone out.

## Safety notes

* The lexicon is a verbatim copy of the app's. When the app's file changes, run `npm run sync:lexicon`; the tests fail until the copy matches. Server-only additions live in `src/safety/extra-lexicon.js`: more than a dose of medicine ("took a load of paracetamol", "a handful of tablets", "the whole box of sleeping pills"), cutting and other self-harm ("I've started cutting again", "burned myself on purpose"), not wanting to exist or to have been born, "no point in me being alive", plans and places ("a plan to end it", "on the bridge"), someone else wanting to end their life or cutting, sexual coercion ("forces me to have sex", "doesn't stop when I say no"), touching by an adult ("my uncle touches me"), violence by step-parents and other relatives, and a few idiom guards ("my phone is going to die", "took my meds on time"). Each has a regression test, and so does the everyday wording it must leave alone. Once the app's shared lexicon has them, delete them there.
* Support titles: the shared copy still says "Let's pause the reframe" / "This isn't one to reframe"; until it is reworded, the server sends "Let's pause Another Angle" / "This one needs real support" instead (`src/safety/index.js`), because "reframe" is jargon the gate never lets the model use.
* The model's own `safety_status` is honoured, so subtle crisis wording the lists miss still gets support. The eval measures that on its own (see above).
* Harmful experiences are never reframed, and an ordinary reframe is never offered as enough support in an emergency.
* The helplines in `support.resources` come from the shared copy: the emergency numbers 999 (UK), 911 (US), 112 (EU) and 000 (Australia); Samaritans (UK & Ireland), 988 (US), and Lifeline, the Suicide Call Back Service and 13YARN (Australia); for care, the UK, US and Australian (1800RESPECT) domestic-abuse lines; findahelpline.com for everywhere else. The server does not guess where the person is; the app's support sheet orders its own lines by the phone's region.
* The word "AI" never appears in what people see: the support copy, the subtitles and the gate all keep to hushmallow's own language.
