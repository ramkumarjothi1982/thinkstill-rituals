# Part A: the guidelines

## Why these exist

Hushmates is a gentle place. Someone writes what's on their mind, lets it go, and up to five other people offer a
different way of seeing it. That only works if everyone is kind, and if everyone knows what Hushmates can and can't
do.

Hushmates is for everyday feelings. In careful words:

> Hushmates is a space for sharing everyday feelings and receiving kind perspectives from other members. It is not
> intended to diagnose, screen for, prevent, monitor, predict, treat or alleviate any disease or mental health
> condition, and it is not a health, therapy or crisis service.

## The heart of it

- **Be kind.** Someone real wrote this, maybe on a hard day.
- **Offer a possibility, not a verdict.** No diagnosis, no judgement, no telling anyone what to do.
- **Keep names out of it.** Leave out names, places and anything that could identify you or anyone else.

## What's welcome

- **Everyday feelings, big and small.** Worry, joy, loneliness, frustration, excitement, the lot.
- **Saying you're having a hard time.** That's okay here. If your words describe something that needs more than a
  new way of seeing it, like not feeling safe, abuse, violence, threats, harassment or being treated unfairly,
  Hushmates won't send them. It shows you support lines instead, and your words aren't saved (see "Writing a feeling"
  and "If someone is in danger right now").
- **Gentle, honest perspectives.** "Maybe…", "I wonder if…", "Another way to see it…".
- **Short and simple.** One kind thought is plenty.
- **Letting a feeling float past.** You never have to catch one, and you never have to explain why.

## What's not okay here

Please don't post any of these, in a feeling or a perspective.

**Unkindness, harassment and hate**
- Insults, mockery, bullying or put-downs.
- Attacking or demeaning people for who they are, such as their race, ethnicity, religion, disability, sex, gender,
  sexuality or age.
- Telling anyone they shouldn't exist, or should hurt themselves.
- Using words about mental health as insults, like "crazy" or "psycho".

**Threats**
- Threatening to hurt anyone, or encouraging anyone else to.

**Sexual content**
- Sexual content of any kind. Hushmates isn't the place for it.

**Content that encourages or explains suicide, self-harm or disordered eating**
- Anything that encourages, praises or dares someone to end their life, hurt themselves, or starve, purge or harm
  their body.
- Anything that describes how, or where. That includes describing methods as a warning, or from your own past.
- Anything that makes suicide or self-harm sound like a solution, a relief or a way out.

Sharing that you're struggling is different, and it's welcome. See "What's welcome".

**Seriously harmful or illegal material**
- Any sexual material involving anyone under 18, including written descriptions. Never, in any form.
- Material that supports or encourages terrorism or violent extremism.
- Descriptions of extreme violence or cruelty meant to shock, thrill or celebrate.
- Instructions for crime or violence, or for making or using illegal drugs.

If you ever see material involving the sexual abuse of a child, **don't copy, screenshot or share it, even to report
it.** Report it in Hushmates (choose "Sexual content"). You can also report it to the Australian Centre to Counter
Child Exploitation (ACCCE) or to eSafety.

**Personal details**
- Names, phone numbers, email addresses, social media handles, street addresses, postcodes, workplaces or schools,
  yours or anyone else's.
- Links, or ways to find someone outside Hushmates.
- Trying to work out who someone is, or sharing other people's private information.

**Scams and spam**
- Asking for money, selling anything, advertising, or promoting a business, a page or a service.
- Repeating the same perspective, or chain messages.

**Pretending**
- Presenting yourself as a counsellor, doctor or other professional, even if you are one. Here, everyone's just a
  person.
- Pretending to be our team, or to be someone you're not.

**Taking words out of Hushmates**
- Copying, screenshotting or posting someone else's feeling or perspective anywhere else. A share picture that
  Hushmates makes for you is fine: it only includes other people's words if they said yes.

**Gaming the system**
- More than one account, getting around a block or a restriction, bots or scripts, or reporting things you know are
  fine just to cause trouble.

## Writing a feeling

- **Write it your way,** in 3 to 500 characters. Add a feeling's name if you like.
- **Leave out names and details.** Words can give people away. Hushmates checks for some details, like phone numbers,
  links, street addresses, postcodes and "my name is…", and will ask you to take them out.
- **It only travels if you say so.** You tick "Share with Hushmates participants, without my name" before it goes.
- **If your words describe something that needs more than a new way of seeing it,** like not feeling safe, abuse,
  violence, threats, harassment or being treated unfairly, or if they tell someone to hurt themselves, Hushmates won't
  send them. It may also hold back words that mention suicide or self-harm at all, even about the past or about
  someone you've lost. Your words aren't saved, so nobody at Hushmates sees them or will be in touch about them.
  You'll see support lines instead, and you can write something else whenever you're ready.
- **The word check can miss things,** even words that clearly mean someone isn't safe. It reads English only, and
  nobody is watching in real time.
- **You stay in charge.** You can bring a feeling home early, let it go, or delete it.

## Writing a perspective

When a feeling finds you, you can catch it or let it float past. If you catch it, you have about 10 minutes to write.

- **Keep it short and kind,** 8 to 280 characters.
- **Offer a possibility, not a verdict.** "Maybe they've had a big week" is kinder than "They're definitely avoiding
  you."
- **No diagnosis, judgement or orders.**
- **Who reads it.** The person who released the feeling reads it when it comes home, and so does anyone who adds a
  perspective after you. Nobody sees who wrote it, and nobody can contact anyone.
- **Share pictures.** You choose whether they may include your words in a share picture or video. It's off unless you
  tick it, and it never shows who you are.
- **You can take it back** at any time in You & safety.

Hushmates checks perspectives too. It won't pass on unkind phrases, words telling anyone to hurt themselves,
diagnoses, advice about food, weight, medication or drinking, personal questions (like someone's age, where they live
or their school), asking to meet up or for pictures, contact details, links, postcodes, requests for money, words about
something that needs real support rather than a new angle, or something almost the same as an earlier perspective. It
may also nudge you gently if you sound very certain. Naming a support line, like Lifeline on 13 11 14, is fine.

### If someone might be struggling

Sometimes a feeling is heavy. If you're not sure what to say, these come from Australian guidance on talking safely
about suicide and hard times.

**First, check in with yourself.** It's okay to let it float past. You don't have to respond to everything, and
looking after yourself matters too.

**If you write something:**

- **Listen first.** Say what you hear. "That sounds really heavy." "It makes sense you'd feel worn out."
- **Don't diagnose or label.** Don't tell anyone what they "have", or what's "wrong" with them. You don't know, and
  it isn't your job.
- **Don't argue with how they feel, or shrink it.** Leave out "at least", "just" and "look on the bright side".
- **Don't tell them what to do.** Leave out "you should" and "you need to".
- **Never mention methods or places,** even to warn them off.
- **Never make suicide or self-harm sound like a way out,** or peaceful. It's best not to name them at all:
  Hushmates holds back perspectives that do, however gently they're put (see below). "That sounds really heavy" says
  enough.
- **Gently point to support.** For example: "Lifeline is there any time on 13 11 14." You can't reach them, but
  they can reach people who can help.
- **Never promise to keep them safe,** or that everything will be okay, or that you'll be there. You can't contact
  them, and they can't contact you. An honest, kind line is enough.

**If you think they might be in danger,** write something kind if you feel able, and also report the feeling with
**"Someone may be in danger"**. It goes to the front of our safety team's queue, and that usually pauses the feeling
straight away. Our team reads reports when it can, not around the clock. It can't see who wrote it, so it can't
contact them or send help. If you know who they are, and they're in danger right now, call **000**.

**If your words mention suicide or self-harm, or suggest someone might not be safe,** Hushmates won't pass them on,
even if you meant them kindly or they're about someone else. It can't tell the difference, so it shows you support
instead, in case this is about you, and the feeling floats on to someone else.

**Afterwards,** be gentle with yourself. If it's stayed with you, Lifeline is there any time on **13 11 14**.

## Looking after yourself as a catcher

- Catching is always your choice. Turn "Let feelings find me" off in You & safety whenever you like.
- Feelings are picked at random from the ones that have waited longest, not from anything about you. Now and then, one
  may be hard to read.
- You can always let it float past, report it, or never see that person's feelings again.
- **Support** is one tap away, any time.

## Reporting and blocking

### How to report

- **A feeling you've been offered or caught:** tap **More** (the three dots), then **Report this feeling**.
- **A perspective that came home to you:** tap **More** on it, then **Report it**.
- **A Mallow perspective:** tap **More** on it, then **Report it**.

Choose what best describes it:

| Reason | Use it for |
|---|---|
| Unkind or judgemental | Put-downs, mockery, harsh judgement |
| Harassment or hate | Bullying, threats, attacks on who someone is |
| Shares personal details | Names, contact details, addresses, anything that identifies someone |
| Sexual content | Anything sexual, including anything sexual involving a child |
| Spam, scam or asking for money | Selling, advertising, links, money requests |
| Someone may be in danger | Words that make you worried someone might not be safe |
| Something else | Anything else that breaks these guidelines, including if you think someone here is under 18 |

You don't have to explain. There's no box to type in.

As soon as you send it, the feeling leaves your sky, or the perspective leaves your collection. Nobody is told who
reported.

### What happens when you report

**Who reads it.** A person on our safety team. We're a small team, **[a few people]** during the beta. We're not
counsellors, clinicians or crisis workers.

**What they see.** The words that were reported, when it was reported, whether the same account has been reported
before, and an account reference, which is a code, not a name. They never see your name or email, the other
person's name or email, or who reported.

**When.** Reports wait in a queue until someone on the team opens it. Nothing alerts the team when a report comes
in, and nobody watches the queue around the clock. Reports that someone may be in danger go to the front. During the
beta we aim to read priority reports within a day, and others within two days. Nights, weekends and holidays can take
longer. That's an aim, not a promise. If the team is away for a while, the message you see after reporting says so,
and when we expect to be back if we know.

**What they can do.**
- Let a feeling carry on, if it breaks no rule.
- Take a feeling out of circulation. Nobody else is offered it, and it goes home to the person who wrote it, with
  whatever came back.
- Remove a perspective. Its words are wiped for good.
- Restrict the account that wrote it, so it can't release or catch feelings, or write.
- For a Mallow perspective, note what went wrong, so the line can be fixed or taken out.

**What they can't do.**
- See who anyone is. So they can't contact anyone, not the person who wrote it and not you, or send help.
- Tell you what they decided. Your Download my data file shows only that a report was "reviewed by our safety team".
- Take back words people have already read, or a share picture someone has already made.

**Automatic pauses.** A feeling is paused straight away if two different people report it, or if one person reports
that someone may be in danger. (If you've reported the same feeling before, a new report from you waits for our team
instead.) While it's paused, nobody else is offered it. It still comes home to its writer when its day is up. Its
writer sees that it's paused for a safety review, but never who reported it, and that our team can't contact them.

### Blocking

You can make sure you never meet someone in Hushmates again.

- **From a feeling:** tap **More**, then **Don't show me their feelings**. From then on, their feelings won't find
  you, and your perspectives won't reach them.
- **From a perspective:** tap **More** on it, then **Never reach me again**. From then on, their perspectives won't
  reach you, and your feelings won't find them. Their words already in your collection are hidden.

Neither of you is told, and neither of you learns who the other is.

## If someone is in danger right now

**Hushmates can't send help. Please don't wait for us.**

**If it's you:**
- If you might act on thoughts of ending your life, or you're not safe, call **000** now.
- If you can, tell someone near you how you're feeling. You don't need the right words first.
- You can talk to someone any time:
  - **Lifeline: 13 11 14**, any time, day or night, or chat online at lifeline.org.au
  - **Suicide Call Back Service: 1300 659 467**, any time
  - **Beyond Blue: 1300 22 4636**, any time
  - **13YARN: 13 92 76**, crisis support by and for Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander people, any time
  - **1800RESPECT: 1800 737 732**, about family, domestic or sexual violence, any time
  - **Outside Australia:** findahelpline.com lists free, confidential helplines in many countries

**If it's someone with you, or someone you know:**
- If they might be about to hurt themselves, or they're in danger right now, call **000**. That comes before anything
  else.
- If it's safe to, stay with them, or keep talking to them.
- You don't have to handle it alone. Lifeline (**13 11 14**) helps people who are worried about someone else, too.

**If it's someone on Hushmates:**
- You can't reach them, and neither can we. Our team can't see who they are.
- Write something kind if you feel able, and gently point to Lifeline.
- Report it with **"Someone may be in danger"**. It goes to the front of the queue, and that usually pauses the
  feeling. Our team reads it when it can, not straight away.
- If you know who they are outside Hushmates, and they're in danger right now, call **000**.

**Finding support in the app.** Tap **Support** at the top of any screen, or go to **You & safety** and choose
**Get support now**, the first thing there. The lines are part of the app, so they still open if your connection
drops.

## If you break these guidelines

We'd rather help than punish. What we do depends on how serious it was, whether it's happened before, and whether
anyone could be hurt.

- **The word check** may stop your words being sent, and tell you why.
- **Our safety team** may take a feeling out of circulation or remove a perspective.
- **For repeated or serious breaches,** we may restrict your account. A team lead may suspend it, or close it.
- **For seriously harmful or illegal material,** our safety team acts on it as soon as they read it, and we report
  it to the authorities when the law requires it.

If your account is restricted, you can still see your journeys, download your data, take back perspectives and
delete everything.

**If you think we got it wrong,** ask for a review: email **[contact email, to be set up]** from the address you sign
in with, with "Review" in the subject. The Terms of Use (section 7) explain how reviews work. If this is a hard time,
Lifeline is there any time on **13 11 14**.
