## What was built

**The score plan (`audio/score.mjs`).** `buildScore({log, direction?, spec?}, {compat, fixes, sound, sync})`
returns `{plan, report}`. It is a pure function of the cue log, the direction, the spec's sound slots,
the library and the hit-sync input.

- **Who sounds, cue to recipe, layers, fixes as flags (`FIX_ORDER = r10, r11, swirl, r7`).** As in round
  2. Compat turns every fix off and refuses any hit-sync input.
- **Hit-sync input (R3).** `opts.sync = { advance: {cueIndex: s}, soften: {cueIndex: {k: dB}}, clearTicks: {cueIndex: true} }`
  takes effect as the recipes emit:
  - advance: the cue's kit gesture starts s earlier (at most `SYNC_MAX` = 0.3 s), its cut moving with it;
  - soften: the k-th studio event of the cue's recipe is lowered by dB;
  - clearTicks: the clock keeps out of that cue's whole hit window, in place of v2's ±80 ms.

  The report lists `kitVoices` (each kit gesture with its cue and advance) and `studio` (each recipe's
  studio events), which the move-finder reads.
- **MO-CLOSE in the phone band (R3).**
  - The eyes-open celesta pair is a figure: `R.pitch.figure(contour.open)` transposes it as one unit and
    lifts it by octaves until its lowest note reaches `PHONE_HZ` = 600 Hz. That is the band phones
    play and the band the gate times hits in. G-rooted kits: D5–G5 → D6–G6. Noomi: v2's G5–C6,
    bit for bit.
  - `phoneClose`: where every audible component of the kit's closing phrase lies below 600 Hz (only
    Loomi: a G3 voice and a low bell), MO-CLOSE's celesta doubles the phrase's first note on the logo,
    in the lowest octave inside the band (196 → 784 Hz).
  - `arrangeClose` (R2) now places its resolution outside every graded cue's hit window (`HIT`: the
    gate's LOUD ∪ QUIET cue types and its [−60, +120] ms window; a unit test holds them equal to
    `qa/gates/audio.py` and `thresholds.json`). Glitzi's and Boppo's resolutions move from 12.855 and
    12.87 s to 12.921 s, just past the sleepy blink's window.

**Hit sync (`audio/hits.mjs`, `audio/hits.py`; new in R3).** A cue's hit is the loudest transient in the
phone band just after the cue. The engine's voices are kit-specific and its master chain compresses, so
the score cannot predict that before it hears it. `scoreToWav` therefore renders and masters, then
`hearHits` runs `audio/hits.py`. That script imports the gate's own detector (decode, 600 Hz high-pass,
2 ms envelope, `onset()`, cue classes, tolerances) and repeats the gate's grading loop; a unit test holds
the two equal. It adds the peak time and the late peak's excess over the cue's first transient.
`hitMoves` turns each late, graded hit into one move:
- **advance**: a kit gesture is sounding at the late onset, owned by this cue or one ≤ 0.3 s earlier. The
  gesture starts earlier by exactly the lateness, so that note lands on the gesture's own cue and its
  first notes lead in. Sound editors line up a transient this way.
- **soften**: a studio note of the cue's own figure starts at the late onset, after its accent. It is
  lowered by the measured excess + 1.5 dB, at most 12 dB.
- **clear**: a clock tick sits at the late onset. v2's clock keeps only 80 ms from a transient cue, which
  is inside the 120 ms window.

Anything else (early hits, a studio note that is not an answer, an advance past 0.3 s) is reported
and never hidden: the check fails. The loop runs at most `HIT_ROUNDS` = 6 renders and reuses the bed's
crossfade statistics between rounds. It is deterministic.

**The loop's splice (`audio/splice.mjs`; new in R3).** On a continuous loop the gate's raw step is the
slope at the cut. Measured on T01's default score, the slope near the seam has an RMS step of −48 dBFS:
the bed alone −50.7, the cue sounds and the sleeping breath −51.5, every other layer below −65
(`/home/user/hm-work/wp04r3/scratch/stems.mjs`). Within ±1 ms of the seam, 14 % of the points read under
−60 and 5 % under −66 (`rot.py`). Shaping the content cannot get the seam to −60 without a dip of
≥ 12 dB in the bed and the breath, which is the hole R7 removed. The score instead:
1. takes the mastered loop at 24-bit precision and ranks every shift within ±48 samples by its raw
   wrap step;
2. keeps the shifts that leave the gate's global A/V offset at 0 (JS port, `globalOffset`) and that are
   already ≤ −63 dBFS in the WAV;
3. encodes the flattest of these exactly as `muxLoop` will (first 2 frame alignments), and accepts the
   first whose delivered raw step is ≤ −63 dBFS, click < −60 and offset 0;
4. writes the shifted loop as `score.wav` and records `plan.circular.splice` and `report.splice`.

Every score so far passed on the first candidate. A/V moves by ≤ 1 ms, against the gate's 20 ms
tolerance for a loud hit.

**Loop-aware AAC (`audio/loop-aac.mjs`).** As in round 2, but `encodeLoopDelivery` now accepts a frame
alignment only when the decoded seam passes both checks the gate reads: raw step ≤ `SEAM_STEP_TARGET`
(−63 dBFS) and click < −60.

**Checks (`audio/check.mjs`).** As in round 2, plus:
- `globalOffset`: a port of the gate's onset-strength cross-correlation, held equal to the gate by a
  unit test, with its lag-0 margin;
- `gAudio`: adds `offset` (on the delivered audio) and `onsets` (the final hits);
- `closeCheck`: reads the logo's gesture from `report.kitVoices`, so a synced gesture still counts.

**Running (`audio/score-cli.mjs`).** `scoreToWav` runs the hit-sync loop, then the splice for a
circular plan, then hears the final hits. `--check` adds the delivered offset and the onsets. A farm
job's `score-plan.json` carries `sync`, `splice` and the final hits. The summary prints each move and
the splice.

**Farm (`render/mux.mjs`, WP-01's file, edited as its header asks: "WP-04 adds its generalised score
here").** `SCORES` gains `'hm-score': 'audio/score.mjs'`. `muxJob` muxes a job loop-aware (`muxLoop`)
when `score-plan.json` says the plan is circular, and keeps the plain mux otherwise.

**Lint (`audio/lint-shapes.mjs` new, `audio/lint-recipes.mjs`).** The detectors are summarised in finding
2 above; the module header gives each rule's reasoning. Every limit is in `SHAPE`.
- The jump-scare limits (`LOUD`) are v2's maxima + 3 dB, recomputed by a test over every v2 recipe and
  the approved v2 score:
  - rise within 50 ms and within 250 ms: 0.0546 (the landing) → 0.0773;
  - bright broadband noise: 0.0110 → 0.0155;
  - engine voices stacked: v2's 0.8 → limit 0.9.
- An engine voice is ranked against the studio's synth at a representative level (`ENGINE.eff`), so a
  quiet engine voice cannot hide a beep.
- `tap`, `press` and `hop` are the Mallow's only body-thump gestures.

**Unchanged from round 2:** transposition (`audio/pitch.mjs` gains only `figure`); the layers bar the
clock's busy spans (`audio/layers/tick.mjs`); the kit voice models (`library/sound/soundmap.json`); the
renderer bar `writeWav24` and bed-statistics reuse (`audio/render-offline.mjs`).

**Review gaps still open:**
1. **Criterion 5: the lint does not reject a ringtone cadence.** `ringtone()` (`audio/lint-shapes.mjs`)
   looks only for a melodic figure of ≥ 2 distinct signal notes played again, and the beep-beep rule
   needs beeps ≤ 0.25 s. A dual-tone burst cadence is not modelled, so a UK ring (400+450 Hz, 0.4 s on,
   0.2 s off, 0.4 s on, also over two cycles), a US ring (440+480 Hz held 2 s) and a 20 Hz trill bell are
   accepted, and `assertLinted`/`buildScore` would play them.
2. **Criterion 5: "steady" is decided from the raw glide in cents**, ignoring the glide's duration and
   energy (`synthPart`: `steady: Math.abs(glide) <= SHAPE.steadyCents`). An inaudible 2 ms, 110-cent onset
   scoop under a 4 ms attack turns any beep into a non-signal, although the module's own energy-weighted
   pitch is unchanged (879.6 vs 880 Hz). Every signal-note detector then skips it: two-tone and tri-tone
   pops, beep-beep, the 3-beep train and the scooped Nokia figure all pass.
3. **Criterion 5: engine voices are always unpitched in the lint** (`enginePart`: `pitched: false,
   steady: false`). Pitched engine instruments (`pluck` and `drip` take a pentatonic `note`; `chime`
   and `glass` take `pitch`) are invisible to the ringtone, pop and alarm detectors, and `isThump`
   accepts an engine-only cluster only for the Mallow's tap, press and hop, so a lub-dub on the engine
   `thrum` (a 98 Hz sine swell) passes too.
4. **Heartbeat limits are tested only at their exact boundary.** A thump with a 0.36 s decay tail
   (limit 0.35 s, measured on the −60 dB tail), a 55 ms attack (limit 50 ms) and a 270 Hz lub-dub pass.
5. **Delivery risk, not in the open issues above.** The loop-aware MP4 hides 1 s of pre-roll audio
   behind its edit list, with `avoid_negative_ts` disabled. A player or transcoder that ignores MP4 edit
   lists plays the score 1.02 s late against the picture (the reviewer's `editlist.py`: the WAV sample at
   5.0 s decodes at 6.0213 s). Platforms re-encode uploads, so this needs a check on a real upload
   (WP-25/WP-27) or a mux that needs no edit list. Raised in OWNER-DECISIONS Q7.
6. **G-AUDIO does not call this lint.** WP-05's `recipes` check (`qa/gates/audio-gate.mjs`) matches recipe
   and event *names* against the banned list; the structural lint runs only inside `buildScore`. A score
   made outside `audio/score.mjs` is therefore not shape-checked by QA. Wiring `lintEvents` over the score
   plan into G-AUDIO belongs with the follow-up for gaps 1–4, so the gate does not inherit the evasions.
