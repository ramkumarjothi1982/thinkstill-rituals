## What was built

### 1. Quality profiles (`engine/render/profiles.js`)

| Profile | What it is | Settings | Per 15 s (measured, 1 worker) |
|---|---|---|---|
| `exact` (default) | the approved v2 pipeline; goldens and parity only | the v2 shader text byte for byte, the direction's own sub-frame count (floor 3), the face redrawn per sub-frame with full-resolution blurs; x264 QP 15 (WP-01) | 24.73 min (`hm bench`) |
| `final` | the production master | every feature below at scale 1; x264 QP 15 master (segment-invariant, WP-01) + CRF 20 preview (x264 fast) | **10.12 min** (farm wall clock) |
| `draft` | review drafts | scale 0.5 (uRes stays in design space), 1 sub-frame, the face once per frame without glow, 3-octave world without clouds, no 1/8 bloom, x264 veryfast QP 20, DRAFT mark | **95.1 s** (farm wall clock) |
| `proof` | a quick look | 6 stills with the final profile's settings (`hm proof`) | **14.7 s** |

Every optimisation is a named **feature** that can be switched off on its own:

- **Where:** `--features -worldFast` on `hm render`, `hm bench` and `hm bench gate`, or `features=` on the engine page.
- **Why:** each step's pixel gate compares the final profile with and without its feature, and a regression can be bisected.
- **Dependent features:** a feature that reads another one's output follows it off. `faceFastGlow` needs `faceCache`, `worldPasses` needs `worldFast`, `tightPasses` needs `fgAccum`. Asking for one without its base is an error.

**Quality knobs.** The draft's cut-downs are knobs in `profile.quality`: `subframes`, `faceGlow`, `worldOctaves`, `worldClouds`, `bloomEighth`. Every other profile runs them at full quality.

**Farm integration.** The profile is part of the farm's job key. It supplies the defaults of scale, encoder, corner mark and preview; explicit options still override them. The engine page, `hm render`, the farm and `hm bench` all take `profile` / `features`. The default stays `exact` (see Deviations).

### 3. The optimisation steps (features of the final profile)

- **P2 `overlaySkip`** (`engine/render/overlay-ink.js`): part of R21.
  - The overlay context is wrapped: painting marks it inked, and a whole-canvas clear marks it clean.
  - A clean overlay is neither cleared again, uploaded nor composited.
- **P3 `faceCache`** (`engine/face/cache.js`, `engine/renderer/passes/face.js`): fixes R1.
  - **Quantised key:** face parameters are quantised with quanta derived from the face spec. One quantum moves no drawn point more than 0.25 art px, or an opacity by more than 1/510.
  - **Pure function of the key:** the face is drawn *from* the quantised values, so its canvas depends only on the key.
  - **Mouth wave:** `mouthWavePhase` is dropped while `mouthWave < 0.01`, and taken modulo 2π otherwise.
  - **Rest:** at rest (≥ 0.999 on every sub-frame), the quanta are 8× coarser.
  - **When it draws:** once per frame, at the centre time. Per sub-frame only while the face moves more than 1.5 output px (blinks, eye pops). On a key hit it is neither drawn nor uploaded.
  - **No render history:** frames no longer depend on earlier renders, so segment pre-roll replays nothing.
- **P3b `faceFastGlow`** (`engine/face/tube.js` `fastTube`, `engine/face/glow.js`):
  - The tube's blurred layers are drawn at reduced resolution.
  - The face is drawn into a canvas the size of the face box and uploaded as the dirty rectangle.
- **P4 `worldFast`** (`engine/worlds/glsl/sky.js`):
  - Each sky term is guarded by its zero region.
  - The sky is rendered once into a half-float layer, and the water samples it instead of evaluating `sky()` again.
- **P5 `fgAccum`** (`engine/renderer/frame-fast.js`):
  - Premultiplied foreground accumulation: `accum = Σ w·FG + world·(1 − Σ w·α_FG)`.
  - Every pass is scissored.
  - The mirror image is drawn at 1/4 resolution from a 48×48 mesh.
- **P6 `adaptiveFloor`** (`engine/render/shutter.js`):
  - One centred sample when nothing moves 2 px or more while the shutter is open.
  - v2 goes from 1,991 to 1,611 sub-frames.
- **P7 `meshNormalTex`** (`engine/renderer/passes/normals.js`):
  - Rest normals are baked once per Mallow.
  - The mesh index is trimmed to the cells that can show anything (R21): 17,114 of 18,432 cells kept.
  - `SoftBody.R`, which was never used, is dropped (R21).
- **P8:** measured; flags unchanged. `hm bench --chromium-args` runs any flag set on top of the engine's, and records it in the result.
- **P4b `worldPasses`** (`engine/worlds/glsl/sky.js`, `engine/worlds/lake.js` `renderSplit`).
  - **Why:** probing showed that SwiftShader evaluates both sides of a branch.
    - The sky pass cost the same with or without its moon, hills and star code: 40–50 ms without them against 130–150 ms with them.
    - The world pass cost as much on a sky pixel as on a water pixel: 77–89 ms on water rows only, 150–183 ms on the whole frame.
  - **Smooth sky at 1/2 resolution:** away from the moon, `sky() = hills(P + (1 − a)·stars)`. The smooth part `P` and the cloud weight `a` are evaluated at 1/2 resolution.
  - **Sharp terms where they are:** stars, hills and the moon are drawn in scissored passes over their own rows or box. Around the moon, `sky()` is evaluated term by term.
  - **Water:** the water shader runs only from the horizon's row down. The rows above are copied.
- **P5b `tightPasses`** (`engine/renderer/frame-fast.js`, `passes/filters.js`):
  - The glass backdrop and blur, and the mirror image's blur, are computed only where they are read. These scissors are exact by construction.
  - Single-sample frames composite FG directly instead of going through the accumulation target.
- **Preview:** `crf20` runs x264 `fast` with 4 threads, after the render workers have finished.

**draft** combines these settings:

| Knob | Draft value | What it changes |
|---|---|---|

- It renders 6 stills with the final profile's settings in one engine page: the frames nearest the middles of six equal parts of the film.
- It writes PNGs plus `proof.json`, which holds its timing.
- It takes one shared heavy-lock slot.

### 5. Measuring tools

- **`hm bench`** (`tools/bench.mjs`):
  - Runs under the exclusive heavy lock, 3 runs, and reports the median of every number.
  - A timing pass renders and reads back each frame, as a worker does.
  - A fenced per-pass split uses 1×1 `readPixels` fences, timed with the page's real clock.
  - Default sample: 6 windows of 8 frames.
- **`hm bench gate`** (`tools/perf/gate.mjs`):
  - Reports PSNR, face-box PSNR and SSIM on the 16 golden times.
  - The face box is carried through the frame's own mesh.
  - Profiles without the face cache are rendered cold.
- **M0 tooling** (`tools/perf/`):
  - `bench-table.mjs`: the benchmark table.
  - `ab_sheet.py`: the flip sheet.
  - `side_by_side.py`: the full-video side-by-side.
  - `video_metrics.py`: loop seam and flash. With two videos it also reports the relative change and `within2pct`.
  - `rest-test.mjs`.

1. **P1 was WP-01's** (binary readback). WP-02's first commit (`63ff752`) is the bit-identical refactor plus the measuring tools, with the exact-profile baseline.
2. **Three steps beyond P2–P8.** Each has its own commit, bench and gate:
   - **P4b `worldPasses`:** follows up P4.
   - **P5b `tightPasses`:** follows up P5.
   - **Preview preset:** a faster preset for the preview copy.

   They were needed because the first full farm render of the P2–P8 final profile took 12.64 min (`/home/user/hm-work/wp02/renders/final-1`), over the 12 min acceptance limit. The plan's conditional perf pass 2 (WP-47) would have been triggered.
3. **P8 changes no flag.** WP-01 already made the software-WebGL opt-in explicit (R2). The measurement found no candidate faster beyond run-to-run noise, and `--in-process-gpu` crashes the browser. The commit records this and adds `hm bench --chromium-args`.
4. **Master encoder: x264 QP 15, not CRF 16.** This is WP-01's segment-invariant encoder, kept for `final`. The CRF 20 preview now uses preset `fast`: same quality against the master, 39 s faster. The draft uses constant QP 20 with `veryfast`, for the same segment-invariance reason.
5. **The default profile stays `exact`.** `hm render` without `--profile` renders exactly what it rendered before WP-02; the goldens, WP-03's parity tests and WP-01's acceptance all assume this. Production renders pass `--profile final`. Changing `DEFAULT_PROFILE` is a one-line decision for the lead.
6. **P6 measured 1,611 sub-frames per film, not ~1,490.** The engine measures displacement itself, including camera, squash/stretch and the face's own motion.
7. **P7 is smaller than estimated.** Noomi's art fills almost all of its grid, so the trim saves little; the baked normals carry most of the step.
8. **`hm bench`'s film figure is a sample figure.** It is the 48-frame sample's mean ms per frame × 450, with no encoder and no page setup. Since P6 the sample holds fewer sub-frames per frame than the film (3.08 against 3.58), so the figure reads 15–20 % low. The acceptance numbers here are farm wall clocks.
9. **Stretch target missed:** 10.12 min against 9.

## Open issues and notes for later packages

- **Stretch target.** The final profile takes 10.12 min against the 9 min stretch. The largest remaining costs per frame (`hm bench`, fenced) are:
  - `sub.mesh` 211 ms;
  - `world` 188 ms;
  - `sub.composite` 119 ms: the mirror image composited over the water, which recomputes the ripple displacement on every sub-frame;
  - `accum` 91 ms;
  - `post` 91 ms.

  The cheapest next step is probably to write the water displacement once per frame (MRT in the world pass) and let the reflection composite sample it. It is estimated at about 30 s per film, and costs a small PSNR loss because it moves the time from the sub-frame to the frame centre.
- **SwiftShader runs both sides of a branch.** Any shader whose cost hides behind a zero-region guard, such as the approved world shader or the fast shaders of later worlds, saves nothing on this machine. Split such work by region with scissored draws, as P4b does. This matters for WP-14 (Moonmilk world terms) and every new world.
- **`hm bench`'s film figure reads low.** Since P6 the 6×8 sample has 3.08 sub-frames per frame against the film's 3.58. A sample weighted by the dry run's sub-frame counts would predict the farm better. The farm's wall clock is the acceptance number.
- **The default profile is still `exact`.** WP-04 and WP-05, or the lead, decide when production renders switch to `--profile final`.
