# Reflect hero pilots — storyboards and source mapping (Gate B)

Three pilots, three genres, three engines of play. All three run on the same room transport but share no scene, verb,
state machine or finale.

| | Group Think Glitch | Drama Dubbing Booth | Emotional Rollercoaster |
|---|---|---|---|
| Genre | Mystery | Comedy | Spectacle |
| Main verb | Scrub time + aim a spotlight | Time a line + perform a face | Sculpt a track + ride it |
| Engine | One 3D scene rendered through different cameras | A timeline sequencer over a silent animated short | Spline track builder + physics ride |
| Private part | What your camera saw, your evidence, your verdict | Your dubbed cut | Your track |
| Reveal | The cameras fracture, then reassemble from the ceiling | A premiere: every cut, then the same frame side by side | The ride itself, then every track overlaid |
| Finale | Rewind of the splat + four-camera group photo | Red-carpet marquee and director's cut grid | All carts link into one shared loop + on-ride photo |
| Players | 2–4 (companions fill to 3) | 2–4 (companions fill to 3) | 2–4 (companions fill to 3) |

## 1. GROUP THINK GLITCH — "Four cameras. One cake. Who saw what?"

**Source rituals (`rituals.json`):** TS-004 *Missing Camera Angle* (Glitch, mind reading: run impossible camera angles),
TS-274 *Ceiling Camera Replay* (Loopie, perspective locking: replay from a silent ceiling camera), TS-450 *My View Doesn't
Feel Like a View* (Patch, naive realism), TS-060 *Witness Lineup* (Patch, memory certainty), TS-309 *Find One Dissenter*
(Glitch, false consensus). Psychological core: every viewpoint is partial, and partial views feel like the whole truth.

**World:** a rooftop birthday party at dusk. String lights, skyline, balloons, a three-tier cake, a cat. One real 3D scene
(table, cake, guests, balloons, lights) rendered through four camera rigs, so each player literally sees the same event
from a different place.

**The incident (9 s, authored):** Patch slides the cake toward the table edge (1 s); the cat hops onto the chair (2 s);
Rush, carrying drinks, catches a balloon string with a foot (3.5 s) and stumbles; the cat paws the cake (4.2 s); Rush
lunges to save it (4.4 s); splat (4.8 s); Glitch's phone flash fires (5.5 s); every eye turns to Rush's iced hands (7 s).

**Cameras:** DOORWAY (low, warm: Rush stumbling in, the cat hidden behind the cloth), BALCONY (overhead, cool: the cat's
paw, the flash glare), PHONE (Glitch's handheld: the balloon string, then whiteout), DJ BOOTH (behind the cake, coloured
lights: Patch sliding the cake to the edge, the table wobble).

**Sequence**
1. *Hook (0–3 s):* the party in one sweeping establishing shot; the splat cuts to black; "Four cameras. One cake.".
2. *Discover (private):* you get one camera. Drag the jog wheel to scrub time; drag the spotlight across the dusk-dark frame.
   Evidence glints only when your light is on it at the right moment. Tap to pin it: a polaroid develops in your tray (3).
3. *Interact / transform:* the corkboard. Drag your polaroids into BEFORE / MOMENT / AFTER, then pull the red thread from
   the cake to who or what caused it (Rush, the cat, the balloon, Patch, or "can't tell"). Twist the thread to set how sure
   you are. Stamp the seal. Your verdict is sealed on the server.
4. *Collective reveal:* every camera plays the incident at once in slow motion; at the moment of the lunge the screen
   fractures into shards, one per verdict ("4 cameras, 3 stories"). The shards swing up into the ceiling camera — the
   angle nobody had — and the whole chain plays: edge, cat, balloon, lunge. Each player's spotlight path is drawn over it.
5. *Perspective shift:* "Everyone saw a true piece. Nobody had the whole picture." Each player taps what surprised them.
6. *Finale:* time rewinds; the cake un-splats into the cat's paws; the cast poses and all four cameras flash a group photo
   that develops as four polaroids. Share card: cameras and verdict icons only, no names unless everyone consents.

**Companions:** Glitch (picks the evidence nearest the cake, literal verdict), Sync (picks faces and reactions), Loopie
(picks the most dramatic frame). Their spotlight paths are scripted and visible in the reveal, labelled.

## 2. DRAMA DUBBING BOOTH — "One silent scene. Your voice decides what it means."

**Source rituals:** TS-047 *Same Text, Three Voices* (Patch, tone inference), TS-064 *Wrong Voice Casting* (Loopie,
cognitive fusion), TS-146 *Punctuation on Trial* (Patch, digital tone bias), TS-159 *Two-Lane Conversation* (Patch,
intent vs impact), TS-148 *Ask or Telepathy?* (Patch, communication mismatch). Core: the same actions carry wildly
different meanings depending on tone, timing and face.

**World:** a 1950s-style dubbing studio: a booth window onto a projector screen, VU meters, a mixing desk, tape reels.
On screen: a silent golden-hour park short, *The Gift* (12 s, four beats): Patch arrives with a wrapped box; holds it out;
Sync opens it — a tiny cactus; Patch walks away. Bodies move; faces and voices are blank.

**Sequence**
1. *Hook:* the reel clatters into the projector; the short plays silent; the booth light turns red: "Your voice decides".
2. *Discover (private):* scrub the reel. For each beat, a tray of lines in four tones (warm, sarcastic, nervous, deadpan),
   or type your own (40 characters, optional).
3. *Interact / transform:* drag a line onto a character's lane at the exact frame — timing is the joke. Turn the voice
   knobs (pitch, pace). Perform the face: drag on the expression wheel and the Bubble's face changes live on screen.
   Hit PREVIEW to watch your cut with your synthesized Bubble voices. Seal the cut.
4. *Collective reveal — the premiere:* a cinema with every player in the seats. Each cut plays with a title card
   ("A film by …"). Seat-side buttons (laugh, gasp, aww) send live reactions that float up from the real players.
5. *Perspective shift:* freeze on the moment the box opens: every cut side by side, the same frame meaning four different
   things.
6. *Finale:* the director's cut — all cuts play at once in a grid, perfectly synced — then the red carpet: the marquee
   lights each cut's auto-title, flashbulbs on the cast. Each player chooses whether their cut appears on the share poster.

**Companions:** Loopie (melodramatic lines, big faces), Glitch (deadpan, literal), Rush (always early on the cue).

## 3. EMOTIONAL ROLLERCOASTER — "Same day. Same ride. Totally different drops."

**Source rituals:** TS-209 *Middle of the Movie* (Glitch, peak-end bias), TS-293 *Loud Now, Long How?* (Sync,
intensity-duration confusion), TS-262 *Shuffle Three Scenes* (Loopie, narrative order), TS-055 *First Ten Seconds Bet*
(Patch, affective forecasting). Core: the same events land at different heights for different people, and the peaks
we remember are not the whole ride.

**World:** a theme park at sunset: a coaster that the players build. A fictional Saturday with five moments (alarm
doesn't go off; friend cancels brunch; a stranger compliments your jacket; the group chat goes quiet after your message;
pizza arrives early). Optional: the host enters up to five moments of a real day the group shared (sealed to the room).

**Sequence**
1. *Hook:* the lift-hill chain clanks, the carts crest, the camera tips over the drop: "Same day. Different drops."
2. *Discover / build (private):* five track segments in side view. Drag each segment's peak up or down for how it felt;
   swipe its module: LOOP, DROP, TUNNEL, CORKSCREW, SMOOTH. A test cart runs your track with real physics as you shape it.
   Seal the track.
3. *Interact — the ride:* all riders launch together. You ride your own track from behind the cart: banking, speed from
   the drops, scenery transforming per moment (tunnel darkness, loop fireworks, drop wind). Other riders' carts run on
   parallel tracks beside you, rising and falling where they felt differently. At two junctions, pull the switch lever to
   ride a friend's track for the next moment; everyone sees you jump.
4. *Collective reveal:* the station; the camera pulls back to a giant side view with every track overlaid. Divergence
   points pulse; tap one: "Same cancelled brunch. One of you dropped, one of you looped."
5. *Finale:* every track merges into one shared loop; the carts couple and go round together under fireworks; the
   on-ride photo prints with each rider's face at their biggest drop.

**Companions:** Rush (extreme drops and loops), Still (gentle hills), Drop (deep tunnels), with seeded variation.
